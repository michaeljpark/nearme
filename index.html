Strategic Value

Proximity has no digital channel

E-commerce wins on digital targeting. Physical retail wins on proximity. But proximity has no digital channel. Merchants can promote products online, yet can't reach a customer standing 200 meters away with a real-time offer. Shoppers can browse flyers and apps, yet can't see what's actually available around them in the moment.

Near Me is a real-time, geolocation-based deals platform that activates proximity as a digital channel — built to accelerate clearance cycles, optimize inventory turnover, and establish a unified commercial data layer between merchants and shoppers at the moment of intent.

Problem Definition

The channel best suited to physical retail's biggest strength remains underserved

As e-commerce expands, physical retail is losing ground in a way that's often overlooked. The gap isn't price or selection — it's the integration between digital channels and physical presence. Online platforms target users based on real-time behavior and intent, while a brick-and-mortar store's strongest natural advantage — being close to the customer right now — has no equivalent digital infrastructure to activate it.

Merchants can promote products online, but they can't reach a nearby customer with a personalized, time-sensitive offer at the moment of intent. Shoppers can't see what's actually available within walking distance right now. Existing tools like flyer apps surface this week's deals at a city level — not what's happening on the street they're standing on. The result is a structural gap where proximity, physical retail's greatest advantage, has no digital infrastructure to activate it.

Competitor Analysis

Structural gaps across four incumbent platforms

Flipp
Flyer Aggregator
  • Discovery model Passive flyer browsing — users must manually search by retailer or category, with no intent-based or proximity-aware surfacing.
  • Decision support Optimizes for deal visibility, not store selection. Provides no guidance on where to go relative to a user's current location or time constraints.
  • Scope Scoped to promotional flyers and coupons. Does not address full grocery planning, real-time inventory states, or live pricing at the store level.
  • Gap No location-in-time activation — cannot convert nearby proximity into a relevant, actionable offer at the moment of intent.
Flashfood
Surplus Marketplace
  • Inventory scope Exclusively serves near-expiry and surplus inventory. The platform's supply logic is reactive rather than predictive, constrained to markdown clearance scenarios.
  • Availability Store-level coverage is uneven and depends on individual retailer participation. Users in underserved markets encounter sparse or absent listings.
  • Use case breadth Does not generalize to everyday retail deal discovery — it serves a narrow salvage use case that excludes standard promotions and clearance-stage pricing.
  • Gap Inventory awareness is reactive and partial; no general-purpose proximity-deal layer exists within the platform.
Many Penny
Deal Aggregator
  • Navigation utility Surfaces deals without guiding users toward an optimal store choice. The experience terminates at discovery, not at a routing or selection recommendation.
  • Data integration Weak coupling to live store conditions — pricing and availability are not validated against real-time inventory or point-of-sale systems.
  • Location intelligence Limited use of geolocation as an active signal. The platform does not rank or filter offers based on proximity, dwell context, or user trajectory.
  • Gap No intelligence layer connecting offer relevance to user location, time, or store-level conditions.
Foodhero
Discounted Food Listings
  • Location model Provides location-based listings of discounted food items, but proximity is used as a filter, not as an optimization signal for store selection.
  • Decision support Does not help users evaluate which store represents the best overall option given their location, basket, or time availability.
  • Scope Constrained to discounted food; no cross-category coverage or generalized proximity-deal infrastructure applicable to broader retail verticals.
  • Gap Location is a passive input rather than an active driver — the platform lacks a proximity-first ranking or recommendation engine.

Key Screens

Designed around the user, not the map

Location is the core of NearMe — but a location-first product doesn’t have to be a map-first one. Each key screen is built on a single principle: the user’s position should power decisions, not dictate the first thing they see.

Home
01 · Home

Preference and live deals first — the map is a destination, not the door

Opening straight into a map forces orientation before any value lands. So the home screen leads with what the user already cares about — saved preferences and the freshest discounts nearby — while the map stays one tap away in the persistent bottom nav. Location informs the ranking; it never gates the entry.

  • 1Live location header — sets spatial context without demanding map interaction.
  • 2Category & store filters — narrow to preferred items and chains (Metro, Loblaws, No Frills) instantly.
  • 3“You might like” — personalized to saved preferences and the latest markdowns.
  • 4Map in the bottom nav — a deliberate, always-reachable touchpoint rather than a forced first screen.
Map
02 · Map

Location made spatial, on demand

When the user does want to act on proximity, the map turns nearby deals into a distance-based decision — plotting live offers around their position to compare and route. It’s entered intentionally from the nav, so the spatial view serves intent instead of interrupting it.

  • 1Live deals plotted by real distance from where the user stands.
  • 2Tap a pin → deal detail with price and real-time stock before any detour.
  • 3Reachable from any screen via the bottom-nav touchpoint.
List
03 · List

Savings made tangible

Saved items roll up into a running SAVE / Total, turning scattered deals into a visible budget win — directly serving the budget-constrained shopper and giving a concrete reason to come back.

  • 1Tap + on any item to save it to the list.
  • 2Running SAVE and Total reinforce realized value.
  • 3Saved stores feed alerts when their prices drop.
Alerts
04 · Alerts

The trigger that closes the loop

Flyer-change and deal-drop alerts bring the user back at the right moment, completing the habit loop the whole flow is built around — re-entering at the commute-time trigger.

  • 1Notifies on flyer or price changes for tracked items.
  • 2Tied to favourited stores and saved preferences.
  • 3Re-enters the flow at the moment of intent.
Design Strategic Value

A real-time commercial data layer is becoming necessary

Three converging signals suggest the infrastructure Near Me addresses is not optional — it is overdue.

Signal 01
Real-Time Clearance Is Scaling in Narrow Verticals
Flashfood and Too Good To Go prove that location-aware, time-sensitive offers move inventory fast — but each solves only one slice of the problem: near-expiry food. The model works. The platform layer doesn't exist yet.
Signal 02
Hyperlocal Commerce Is Re-Emerging
15-minute city planning, post-pandemic local consumption, and walking-distance discovery are all rising. Digital tools to support these behaviors lag e-commerce by a decade.
Signal 03
Commercial Data Remains Fragmented
Location, inventory, price, and promotion data live in siloed systems. No unifying layer combines them into a real-time, location-aware channel — the infrastructure gap Near Me is designed to close.

Together, these signals point to one conclusion: the infrastructure that unifies real-time, location-aware commerce is about to become essential, and it does not yet exist.

Retailer Partnership

The data only flows through the retailer

The geo-query architecture is only as real as its feed. Public flyers are stale and partial; live, store-level deals and clearance exist only inside the retailer’s own systems. So the platform is built around the retailer as a first-party data partner — not a scraping target.

Retailer provides
  • Live deal & price feed, per store
  • SKU-level stock & clearance / markdown status
  • Store-network locations & hours
⇆
NearMe delivers
  • A proximity channel — reach shoppers in range, in the moment
  • Real-time, attributable store-visit conversion
  • Faster clearance velocity & inventory turnover
  • Shopper intent & demand signals back to the retailer

This is why H-1 leads with an anchor-retailer LOI: the partnership is the data source — the DATA_SOURCE → INGESTION_JOB path in the model below — and Phase II deepens it into the per-store inventory layer.
